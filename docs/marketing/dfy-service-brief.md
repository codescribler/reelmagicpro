## What the DFY service is

A done-for-you variant of ReelMagicPro: the parent sends the founder a Veo link plus instructions (timestamps and "which player is mine"), and the founder uses ReelMagicPro to produce the finished reel and deliver it as an mp4. Player tracking, focus-box crop, slow-mo on the skill moments, optional backing music, optional intro/outro — all the editing the parent would have done themselves in the app.

**The founder is the operator.** The product the parent buys is "a finished reel from the person who built the app."

**Time to deliver per reel:** ~20 minutes end-to-end including comms, for the standard single-source job.

## Why it works (robustness)

Anchored entirely in the existing buyer profile — no new buyer thesis required:

1. **The real competitor is inaction.** Per `buyer-profile.md`: *"It's been so hard to do that it just doesn't get done"* — *"only 3 or 4 the entire season."* A meaningful slice of parents won't open any editor. They'll pay to skip it.
2. **Willingness-to-pay is already high.** Same parents routinely spend £100s/month on coaching, kit, travel, APDC fees. A £79-£249 reel is a rounding error against a season's coaching costs.
3. **Founder credibility is unfair-advantage strong.** JPL (Cassiobury Rangers) + APDC dad. No Fiverr editor can match the "I know what a scout watches because my boys are in the pathway" claim.
4. **The operating cost is bounded.** 20 minutes/reel = ~£237/hr effective at £79. Labour ceiling does not bind at expected volumes.
5. **It feeds the SaaS funnel.** Every DFY customer is a warm prospect for the DIY app the following season, once the app is publicly priced and on general sale. (App pricing is not yet locked — see memory: `reelmagic-app-pricing-unconfirmed`.)

**Honest framing:** DFY is a *second product line* alongside the SaaS, not a replacement. It captures the buyers who would never use the app themselves.

## The offer — three one-off tiers

Pricing is fixed, visible, and per-reel. **No subscriptions, no season passes, no credit packs at launch.** The buyer pays per reel, every time. If they end up ordering eight reels across a season, that's their choice — nothing in the offer asks them to commit beyond the reel they're buying right now.

| | Single Reel | Match Showcase | Compilation Reel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Price** | £79 | £129 | £249 |
| **Length** | up to 30s | up to 60s | up to 90s |
| **Highlights** | 1 | up to 3 | up to 5 |
| **Source** | 1 match | 1 match | up to 4 matches |
| **Music** | — | included | included |
| **Player tracking + slow-mo** | included | included | included |
| **Turnaround** | 24 hours | 24 hours | 72 hours |
| **Revisions** | 1 round | 1 round | 1 round |

**Founding offer (optional, time-limited):** First reel at £49 to seed testimonials and content for the marketing site.

### Why this structure, in one sentence per principle

- **Three tiers, scannable in five seconds.** Premium brands never make the buyer read a feature matrix to know what to buy.
- **One price per tier — no "from £X."** "From" pricing reads cheap and creates negotiation friction.
- **Multi-source is a *tier*, not a surcharge.** Complexity becomes a product (*Compilation Reel*), not a penalty.
- **Music is bundled into the tier, not a tickbox.** "With music +£20" is a Fiverr move.
- **"1 round of revisions included"** beats *unlimited* — *unlimited* reads as iterative/cheap; *included* signals the first cut is meant to be right.

### Pricing rationale (so the copywriter can defend it on the page)

- £79 anchors as specialist (vs Fiverr generalist ~£30-100). Below £79 the offer reads cheap, which clashes with the founder's authority position.
- DIY app (price not yet locked, but will be much lower than DFY) vs £79 DFY single reel — the gap is wide enough to signal a different value proposition (we do it for you vs you do it yourself), not competing offers.
- £249 for *Compilation Reel* sits well below specialist-agency pricing (£250-£400+) while clearly reflecting the higher complexity of multi-source jobs.

## What the DFY landing page must do

The page must answer, in order:

1. **What is this and who is it for?** A football parent who has a Veo download and wants a scout-ready reel without learning to edit.
2. **Why trust this person to make it?** The founder built the app. JPL + APDC dad. Knows what a scout watches.
3. **What exactly do I get?** A finished mp4 with tracking, slow-mo on the skill moments, optional music, brand outro, delivered in 24 hours.
4. **How do I order?** Pick a tier. Send the Veo link + timestamps + which player is mine. Pay. Reel back in 24 hours.
5. **What does it cost?** The three-tier table, visible above the fold or one scroll down.
6. **What if I'm not happy?** One round of revisions included; clear, fair revision policy in plain language.

## Positioning — "simple but premium"

The DFY landing page must read **simple but premium**, not cheap, not Fiverr, not bargain-hunter. Concrete copy rules:

- **No bullet-point feature lists longer than 5 items** anywhere on the page. Premium brands compress; cheap brands enumerate.
- **No exclamation marks. No emoji. No urgency timers.** The buyer profile lists fake urgency as an explicit anti-feature.
- **No "starting at" pricing.** Tier prices are fixed and visible.
- **Plain English, short sentences.** Same voice as the existing homepage copy — no agency tone, no "elevate your child's brand" language.
- **Founder voice, founder credibility.** Refer to the founder as a real person where it helps trust, but never write fake-customer testimonials (see `No-Fakes Pledge` in `features.md`).
- **Use the buyer's own quotes where they fit.** *"Tracking a moving player is extremely complicated"* — *"Only 3 or 4 the entire season"* — these are headline-grade and already canon.

---

## Constraints and anti-features (do NOT include on the page)

Carried forward from `buyer-profile.md` and `features.md`:

- **No subscription, no season pass, no credit packs.** This was the founder's explicit decision (2026-05-13). The page must read as per-reel and only per-reel.
- **No cloud-upload of footage** other than the Veo link the parent already shares. Footage is handled per-job; nothing accumulates.
- **No fabricated testimonials, fake user counts, or scout endorsements** until they are real.
- **No fake urgency** ("only 3 slots left this week"). Real scarcity or none.
- **No music library.** Parents supply their own mp3. This avoids rights ambiguity.
- **No claims that overlap with the SaaS app's "your footage stays local" promise.** The DFY service is *different* — by definition the parent is sending the founder the link. Be honest: *"You're sending the link to the founder, who edits on his machine. The video is deleted after delivery."*
- **No bait-and-switch on price.** All three tier prices visible. No "contact us for pricing" anywhere.

## Risks and mitigations (so the page can address them where appropriate)

1. **Veo access friction.** Some Veo links are public; some need a club login. → The intake form must explain both link-based routes (Veo share link, or a Drive/Dropbox/WeTransfer link the parent generates from their own cloud). The founder does NOT accept direct file uploads; the page must make this clear so parents do not arrive expecting an upload field.
2. **The first 5 reels set the standard.** → Not a copy concern; an operational note. The page should not promise a turnaround the founder can't honour while still on the early-customer learning curve.
3. **"What if I don't like it?"** → The single round of revisions is the on-page answer. Phrasing must be premium ("one round of revisions included") not transactional ("if you're not happy…").
4. **Buyer worry about footage privacy.** → State plainly that footage is deleted after delivery; not stored, not used in marketing without explicit written permission.
